# Schematic operating requirements

This document defines requirements for drawing, modification, and result checks. The project supplies the confirmed scope, expected connections, and design conditions.

Manage stage acceptance and project records according to the project agreement. For online commands, read the corresponding command reference along with the skill entry and follow help from the installed version. Offline generation and other backends should follow only the applicable drawing and design requirements; they do not authorize online writes.

## Functional-block layout and pin-wiring preparation

- Plan the location, reading order, and title space for each block before drawing a multi-block sheet.
- Size block frames with allowance for actual symbol, pin-lead, label, and attribute-text extents.
- Reserve space based on actual occupied area for large modules and high-pin-count devices.
- Adjust component spacing to symbol size; do not reuse fixed coordinates from another project.
- Before batch placement, prepare part numbers, expected designators, and the pin-to-net table.
- Reuse component information already obtained for a part number.
- Prefer batch lookup when the current commands support it.
- Identify pin nets by component identity and pin number; distinguish units for multi-unit components.
- Check the `refMap` returned by `buildBlock`, then compare actual designators and pin connections.
- List power, ground, signal, and valid NC pins explicitly.
- Verify the purpose of any unassigned pin first.
- Prefer direct wiring between nearby pins on the same net.
- When labels are needed, extend a short wire outward from the actual pin connection point.
- Adjust label spacing for the current units, text, and nearby pins; do not apply the upstream fixed 10-unit offset universally.
- After generation, check that components, labels, and text remain inside their assigned frames; adjust the layout or enlarge the frame if needed.

These requirements guide planning and readback checks. They do not mean `buildBlock` automatically avoids collisions based on symbol size. Adjust existing drawings only within the authorized scope; do not rearrange the whole drawing just to fit functional frames.

Only its layout and batch-preparation methods are used here. Execution still uses this plugin's commands and existing save-acceptance workflow.

## Components and footprints

- Check pin numbers, pin names, electrical types, and hidden pins.
- Ensure multi-unit symbols correspond to the same physical component.
- For a component transferred to PCB, map every symbol pin to a footprint pad.
- A footprint may have extra pads; identify their purpose.
- Do not require the symbol pin count to equal the pad count.
- Check non-transfer symbols, such as net identifiers, according to their purpose.
- Verify model, parameters, part number, and footprint.
- Check BOM inclusion, PCB conversion, and assembly status.
- A 3D model does not prove the pin mapping is correct.

## Schematic drawing

Apply these requirements to new drawings and later refinement. Use the user's default preferences for direct-wire length and placement; explicit project requirements take precedence. Drawing distances do not represent PCB electrical distances.

### Functional regions and net names

- Group by function and arrange in reading order.
- Keep repeated channels functionally consistent and check each one separately.
- Use one clear name for each electrical net.
- Do not place different net names or labels on the same wire.
- When different names are directly connected, first check the originally intended connection.
- If they are the same net, standardize the name.
- If different names must remain, specify how and where the connection is made.
- Do not use flexible mode to connect multiple names to a net as a routine drawing method.
- For a main sheet and sub-sheets, verify the correspondence between ports and nets.
- Local, port, and global names may differ, but their mapping must be correct.

### Direct wiring and labels

- Within one functional region, prefer direct wiring between nearby pins on the same net, including GND.
- Use labels or ports as needed for cross-region, cross-page, and long-distance connections.
- Use connection points recognized by the software.
- Make non-connected crossings clear; do not overlap different nets collinearly.
- By default, a direct wire path should be no longer than 40 grid units.
- Confirm the current grid step before converting coordinates to a length.
- Sum all segments along a path; do not apply the 40-unit limit separately to each segment.
- For branches, take the longest of the shortest connection paths between every pair of endpoints.
- Endpoints include pins, label-stub ends, and branch ends.
- Do not treat the sum of every branch as one path.
- For paths longer than 40 grid units, use a same-name net label.
- Even within 40 units, avoid other nets, symbols, and text.
- Place net labels at clear short-wire endpoints, not directly on pins.
- After placement, verify the actual attached wire and net name.
- After directly joining nearby GND pins, usually keep one label per group.
- Do not leave unnecessary floating wire tails.

### Components and branches

- Orient resistors and capacitors to match the direction of the pins they connect to, reducing detours.
- Capacitors in parallel on an input or output may be placed vertically beside the corresponding side of the IC.
- Connect capacitor power pins to a short bus and join ground locally.
- Keep upper and lower buses close to components while leaving enough room for text.
- Check actual pin locations and polarity during placement; do not add long wires just for alignment.
- If there is not enough room for a component between two pins, prefer a direct connection to the positive or signal side.
- In that case, connect the negative or ground side with a short wire and a label.
- If space and routing allow, connect both ends directly nearby.
- Place termination resistors and similar branches on the relevant interface side; verify each pin's original net name.
- Do not create a long detour or cross other pins just to connect both ends directly.

### Leads and spacing

- Follow the symbol's actual orientation: extend from the real pin endpoint outward first, then turn.
- If the destination is on the other side of the symbol, route around the outside.
- Do not route backward through the symbol body, other pins, or NC markers.
- Do not require a fixed pin number to exit left or right.
- Plan component spacing and bus positions before drawing wires.
- Avoid unnecessary large gaps between interfaces, capacitors, and branches.
- When functional frames are used, keep components and text inside their assigned frame.
- Do not sacrifice text spacing or clarity to shorten wires.
- Add necessary bends to avoid obstacles.

## Labels and NC

- In EasyEDA Pro, a net label sets the wire's name attribute.
- Check the current drag setting before moving a label.
- After dragging, verify whether the original wire name changed.
- NC means a pin has been confirmed as allowed to remain floating.
- Handle unused function pins according to the datasheet.
- Do not use NC to hide an unexplained floating pin.
- In EasyEDA Pro, NC is a pin attribute; place its marker at the pin endpoint.
- Clear the NC attribute before connecting a pin that was marked NC.

## Programming pads, test points, and 0-ohm links

- Draw the signals, reference ground, and target voltage required by the selected programming method.
- Include required reset and boot-configuration connections.
- Check the programming route separately for each programmable device.
- Check whether other components occupy or interfere with programming pins.
- Check whether the programmer and board supply could back-power each other.
- Give test pads clear designators, nets, and footprints.
- State the purpose of each test point and, where needed, its normal voltage or signal range.
- Specify the reference ground used during testing; do not connect grounds across an isolation barrier.
- Draw 0-ohm resistors as actual components and label both nets and their purpose.
- If the link must be opened for measurement, check whether another path still connects its ends.
- State the default assembly status of 0-ohm resistors and how to restore them after testing.
- Bare test pads must transfer to PCB but should not be treated as components to procure or assemble.
- Include 0-ohm resistors and test terminals that are actually installed in the BOM according to assembly requirements.

## Modifications and batch operations

Follow the [schematic workflow](commands-schematic.md) for modification, deletion, abnormal recovery, saving, and readback.

- After moving, rotating, or mirroring a component, read back its pin endpoints.
- A layout-only change must preserve electrical connections and component parameters.
- Before modifying a shared wire, identify all branches and any connections outside the authorized scope.
- When replacing a component, recheck its full set of properties and assembly status.
- Editing a value in EasyEDA Pro may clear the supplier part number; verify it again afterward.
- The number of wire segments does not replace a connectivity check.

## Reusable modules

- Record the source version, operating conditions, and checks already completed.
- Mark unfinished interfaces as pending; do not treat an unfinished module as production-ready.
- Check logic levels, direction, power domains, and unpowered state.
- Check pin order, polarity, footprint, surrounding components, and return current path.
- For a sub-sheet module, check its ports to the main sheet.
- Verify one unit first, then check each designator and channel net.
- Recheck pins and geometry after rotating or mirroring an instance.
- A reusable schematic passing review does not mean its PCB can be copied directly.
- Recheck affected instances after modifying the source module.

## Compare before and after changes

Use the [schematic workflow](commands-schematic.md) to compare component properties, nets, and positions before and after modification. A layout-only change must preserve connections and parameters; check each electrical change against its requirement. Record unexplained differences as pending verification.

## Schematic completion checks

- Check that the programming interface, required test points, and removable 0-ohm links are present.
- Record clearly when an existing interface is reused or an item does not apply.
- Compare every pin against the connection table agreed in advance.
- Check required and prohibited connections, NC status, polarity, and connector pin order.
- Check electrical types, floating pins, duplicate designators, and required attributes.
- Check multi-unit components, net names, and footprint associations.
- Run electrical and design rule checks (ERC/DRC).
- Confirm which pages, components, and rules were included in the checks.
- Explain any excluded objects.
- Do not hide a problem by disabling BOM or PCB inclusion.
- For every warning, state whether it was fixed, the reason it is accepted, or that it remains unresolved.
- Rerun affected checks after changing their rules.
- After each functional region is complete, zoom in and inspect components and every branch.
- Check each pin's lead direction, connection points, and clearance from symbols, NC markers, and text.
- Confirm that the viewed image is from after the modification, not a cached old image.
- A whole-page thumbnail cannot replace local inspection.
- After netlist and DRC pass, still inspect wires crossing symbols and text overlap.
- Finally, inspect full-page regions and reading order.
- After saving, confirm checks refer to the current version.

## Transfer to PCB

- Verify the project, board, schematic, target PCB, and assembly version.
- In EasyEDA Pro, the schematic and PCB must belong to the same board for synchronization.
- Save before synchronization and check the selected items in the change list.
- If rules must transfer, check “Include design rules” and its specific options.
- After selecting “Apply Changes,” inspect the result.
- Check each component, designator, footprint, pad net, and pin order.
- Do not combine different boards or assembly versions into one manufacturing list.
- If a routed PCB's net changes, inspect existing tracks, vias, branches, and copper areas.
- Rebuild copper and rerun checks as needed for the change.
- Successful synchronization does not prove old copper or tracks comply with the new net.
